Coming to America

The seven brothers came over one by one between 1865 and 1872: Peter first, John last. This is their journey and their first years as the book tells it, then the family’s moves to Illinois, California, Oklahoma and Kansas. Every entry cites the book’s own words; where the book disagrees with itself, both readings are shown.

Leaving their small villages they left by train for Bremen, Germany where they boarded ship, arriving in Baltimore in three or four weeks.

p. 7

Our people came west by train as far as Fremont or North Bend, Nebraska, where they forded and Platte River into Saunders County.

The 1860s

  1. 1865 or 1866

    Arrival

    Peter, the first to leave

    Peter, the eldest brother, leaves Bohemia with his wife Anna and their three children, Anna, Joseph and Mary. He goes first to the Czech settlement at Racine, Wisconsin. No ship record was found.

    The sources differ

    • 1865: A family story: Anna Frohner Kastl told her grandchildren they were on a train coming west when Lincoln was assassinated (14 April 1865). p. 7
      As printed
      • Grandmother Anna Frohner Kastl told her grandchildren that Abe Lincoln was assassinated (April 14, 1865) while they were on a train coming west. — p. 7
    • 1866: History of Nebraska (1882) gives 1866. p. 7 p. 15
      As printed
      • Andreas' History of Nebraska, Volume II, page 1400, states Peter came in 1866. — p. 7
      • He emigrated to America in 1866, and located at Cedar Rapids, Iowa, — p. 15

    People: Peter Kastl, Anna Frohner, Anna Kastl, Joseph Kastl, Mary Kastl

    p. 7 p. 8

    As printed
    • Peter was the first to leave. The year is unclear. — p. 7
    • With Peter and his wife Anna were three children: Anna, Joseph, and Mary. — p. 7
    • (He served his time and then the authorities wanted him back in the army so he made a hasty departure to America.) — p. 8
    • He first went to Racine, Wisconsin where there was a Czech settlement. — p. 8
    • We did not find any records on Peter or Jakob. — p. 7

  2. 1866

    Settling in

    Peter in Cedar Rapids, Iowa

    Peter works in the building trade in Cedar Rapids, Iowa.

    People: Peter Kastl

    p. 8

    As printed
    • In 1866 he was in Cedar Rapids, Iowa where he worked in the building trade. — p. 8

  3. 26 September 1866

    Family

    Thomas J. Kastl born in Iowa

    Peter and Anna's son Thomas is born in Cedar Rapids, Iowa.

    People: Thomas J. Kastl

    p. 20

    As printed
    • THOMAS J. KASTL (Peter, Thomas, John, John, Andreas) born 26 September 1866, Cedar Rapids, Iowa — p. 20

  4. 1867

    Background

    Nebraska becomes a state

    The book's account of the brothers in Nebraska begins in the year Nebraska became a state.

    p. 10

    As printed
    • Our account of the Kastl brothers' life in Nebraska begins in 1867, the year Nebraska became a state. — p. 10

  5. 29 May 1867

    Arrival

    Andrew and Thomas, aged 13 and 17

    The two youngest brothers arrive at Baltimore from Bremen, probably at Peter's suggestion. Andrew goes to live with Peter's family; Thomas goes first to Cedar Rapids, Iowa.

    People: Andrew Kastl, Thomas Kastl, Peter Kastl

    p. 7 p. 56 p. 57 p. 10

    As printed
    • The next to arrive May 29, 1867, were Andrew, age 13 and Thomas, age 17. They came on the ship SS Johanne Wilhelmine. — p. 7
    • A few years later, probably at the suggestion of their older brother Peter, young Thomas and Andrew came to the United States, sailing from Bremen, Germany on the S.S Johannes—Wilhelmina, arriving at the Port of Baltimore on 29 May 1867. — p. 56
    • According to ship records, young Andrew and his brother Thomas arrived at the Port of Baltimore 29 May 1867 on the ship Johannes—Wilhelmine which sailed from Bremen, Germany. — p. 57
    • Peter's youngest brother, Andrew, who came from Bohemia in 1867, also lived with the family. — p. 10
    • Family records show that Thomas first went to Cedar Rapids, Iowa and then on to Champaign, Illinois. — p. 56

  6. June 1867

    Homestead

    Peter files for a homestead

    Peter brings his family west by train from Cedar Rapids to Council Bluffs and crosses to Omaha by ferry. On 26 June he files for a homestead at Nebraska City, application No. 1395: 160 acres in Butler County. He is the first Czech settler to apply in the Butler–Saunders area.

    People: Peter Kastl

    p. 10 p. 8 p. 15

    As printed
    • Peter Kastl, the first brother to arrive, came as far as Council Bluffs, Iowa, in 1867, by train from Cedar Rapids, Iowa. With him were his wife and four children. — p. 10
    • On June 26, 1867 Peter was in Nebraska City where he filed for a homestead. — p. 8
    • Peter Kastl was the first Czech settler to apply for homestead in the Butler—Saunders County area. — p. 8
    • His application number was 1395. — p. 8
    • This land was in Butler County, Nebraska. — p. 8
    • where he resided until June 1867, when he came to Nebraska — p. 15

  7. 2 October 1867

    Arrival

    Wenzel and his family

    Wenzel, his wife Barbara and their children arrive at Baltimore on the Humbolt from Bremen (captain Carl Gendes). The ship's list names John, aged 6, Martin, 3, and a Wenzel aged 6 months, who fits no child in the book's family list.

    People: Wenzel Kastl, Barbara Blachnik, John Kastl, Martin Kastl

    p. 7 p. 26

    As printed
    • On October 2, 1867, Wenzel; wife Barbara and three children John, Martin, and Wenzel arrived on the ship SS Humbolt. — p. 7
    • Wenzel and Barbara came to the United States in 1867, arriving at the Port of Baltimore on October 2. — p. 26
    • They left from the Port of Bremen, Germany on the vessel Humbolt, ship's captain, Carl Gendes. — p. 26
    • Listed on the ship's records, as children of Wenzel Kastl, were John, age 6; Martin age 3; and Wenzel, age 6 months. — p. 26

  8. 9 February 1868

    Family

    Philomena Kastl born

    Peter and Anna's daughter Philomena is born, probably in Omaha.

    People: Philomena Kastl

    p. 10

    As printed
    • Philomena was probably born in Omaha, February 9, 1868. — p. 10

  9. 22 April 1868

    Arrival

    Joseph and his family

    Joseph (Josef), his wife Dorothea (Dorota) and their children Mathias, 3, and Theresia, 9 months, arrive at Baltimore on the SS Berlin from Bremen. Joseph first works in Omaha as a stone mason and for the Union Pacific, building station houses.

    People: Josef Kastl, Dorota Blaha, Mathias Kastl, Theresia Kastl

    p. 7 p. 41 Supp. p. 3

    As printed
    • Following Wenzel was Joseph, wife Dorothea and two children Mathias and Theresia, arriving April 22, 1868, on the ship SS Berlin. — p. 7
    • Josef and Dorota sailed from Bremen, Germany on the ship S.S. Berlin, arriving at the Port of Baltimore on 22 April 1868, with two children, Mathias 3, and Theresia 9 months. — p. 41
    • Josef first came to Omaha where he was a stone mason. To raise money for his family, he worked for the Union Pacific Railroad building station houses through Nebraska and Wyoming. — Supp. p. 3

  10. 1 May 1868

    Homestead

    Peter settles near Plasi

    Peter pre-empts 80 acres in Section 20, the land near Plasi where he settles.

    People: Peter Kastl

    p. 8

    As printed
    • On May 1, 1868 Peter pre-empted 80 acres, pre-emption Certificate 1163, N½ SE¼ Section 20, Township 15 North, Range 5 East. — p. 8
    • This is the land where Peter settled in the Plasi vicinity. — p. 8

  11. 20 June 1868

    Homestead

    Joseph files for a homestead

    Joseph files at Nebraska City, application No. 2069: 80 acres in Newman Precinct, Saunders County. He and his wife live in a cave dug into a hillside while he builds a log cabin.

    People: Josef Kastl, Dorota Blaha

    p. 8 Supp. p. 3

    As printed
    • On June 20, 1868, he filed for homestead at the land office in Nebraska City. Application number was 2069. — p. 8
    • He proceeded to build a log cabin, but in the meantime lived with his wife in a nearby cave dug into the side of a hill. — Supp. p. 3

  12. 1868

    Homestead

    The log houses

    Peter moves his family from a dugout into a log house; Joseph builds his 16 by 32 foot log house. The families of Wenzel and Joseph stay in Omaha while the men build.

    People: Peter Kastl, Josef Kastl, Wenzel Kastl

    p. 10 p. 9

    As printed
    • Peter first built a dugout, a place to live, until he finished his log house in 1868. — p. 10
    • According to the Land Certificates, the log cabins were built in 1868. — p. 9
    • The families of Wenzel and Joseph lived in Omaha while the men were erecting their log houses. — p. 10

  13. 1868 or 1869

    Death

    The first deaths in America

    Joseph's two small children, Mathias and Theresia, die soon after arriving and are buried in Omaha.

    People: Mathias Kastl, Theresia Kastl

    p. 10 p. 41

    As printed
    • It was during this time that Joseph's children, Matthias and Theresa, both died, and were buried in Omaha. — p. 10
    • These two children died soon after arriving in Nebraska, and buried in Omaha. — p. 41
    • (d) 1868/9. (bur) Omaha. — p. 41

  14. 1869

    Homestead

    Peter gives up the Butler County homestead

    Peter relinquishes his first homestead for the land near Plasi, with its timber and springs, leaving room for the Czech settlers he knows are coming.

    People: Peter Kastl

    p. 8

    As printed
    • The land that he homesteaded in Butler County was relinquished in 1869. — p. 8
    • also more land was available for further Czech settlers who he knew were soon to come. — p. 8

The 1870s

  1. 1870

    Settling in

    Corn to Nebraska City

    Peter and Joseph haul two wagon loads of corn about a hundred miles to Nebraska City. When someone drives off their oxen, Joseph gets their money with a whip and a shotgun.

    People: Peter Kastl, Josef Kastl

    p. 12

    As printed
    • A trip to Nebraska City by Peter and Joseph was made in 1870. — p. 12
    • He went back with his black snake whip and shotgun, and in strong broken English demanded the money for the corn. — p. 12

  2. Between 1870 and 1880

    Moving on

    Wenzel moves to Omaha

    Wenzel leaves his homestead near Peter's at Plasi for Omaha. The 1880 census finds the family in Douglas County.

    People: Wenzel Kastl

    p. 26

    As printed
    • Wenzel Kastl had a homestead near Peter Kastl at Plasi, but went to Omaha sometime between 1870 and 1880. — p. 26
    • Douglas County Census records for 1880 show children of Wenzel and Barbara Kastl as: John 18, Martin 16, Mary 9, Anna 7, and Wenzel 2. — p. 26

  3. 31 January 1872

    Family

    Jacob's marriage contract

    Jacob and Alzbeta (Elizabeth) Nemecek sign a marriage contract at Lepadlech, Bohemia. Her father promises 250 zlatych toward the trip to America.

    People: Jacob Kastl, Alzbeta Nemecek

    p. 44 p. 45

    As printed
    • probably at her home in Lepadlech, Bohemia where a marriage contract was signed 31 January 1872. — p. 44
    • the sum of 250 zlatych, Two-hundred-fifty Zlatych, with which to make preparations for a trip to America. — p. 45

  4. April 1872

    Arrival

    Jacob and his bride

    Jacob arrives with his new wife, Alzbeta. About three months later they are homesteading on a farm near Plasi. No ship record was found.

    People: Jacob Kastl, Alzbeta Nemecek

    p. 7 p. 44

    As printed
    • Jakob came with his new bride some time in April 1872. — p. 7
    • They emigrated to America — arriving in the United States and homesteading on a farm near Plasi, Saunders county, some three months later — p. 44
    • We did not find any records on Peter or Jakob. — p. 7

  5. 1872

    Background

    The church at Plasi

    The Catholic church in Elk Precinct is organized with 65 members, Peter and Anna among them; Jacob is remembered as one of its founders. The church building follows in 1878.

    People: Peter Kastl, Anna Frohner, Jacob Kastl

    p. 15 p. 44

    As printed
    • He and his wife were among the original members of the Catholic Church in that precinct — p. 15
    • which was organized in 1872 with a class of 65 members — p. 15
    • He was one of the founders of the first Catholic church at Plasi. — p. 44
    • In 1878 they erected their present church — p. 15

  6. 14 August 1872

    Arrival

    John and his family, the last to come

    John, his wife Marie and their four children, Wenzel (Vaclav) 8, Barbara 6, Anna 3 and Josef 11 months, arrive at Baltimore from Bremen. The ship's records give Chicago as their destination.

    The sources differ

    • SS Baltimore: John's chapter names the ship S.S. Baltimore. p. 28
      As printed
      • John and Marie Kastl sailed from Bremen, Germany on the ship S.S Baltimore, arriving at the Port of Baltimore on the 14th of August 1872, with four children, Wenzel 8, Barbara 6, Anna 3, and Josef 11 months. — p. 28
    • SS Berlin: The chapter on the journey names the SS Berlin. p. 7
      As printed
      • John's date of arrival was August 14. With him were his wife, Marie, children: Wenzel 8, Barbara 6, Anna 3, and Josef 11 months. They sailed on the ship SS Berlin. — p. 7

    People: John Kastl, Marie Kunes, Vaclav Kastl, Barbara Kastl, Anna Kastl, Joseph Kastl

    p. 7 p. 28

    As printed
    • The last two brothers, John and Jakob, arrived in 1872. — p. 7
    • It is not known if John and his family stopped in Chicago (ship records indicate that as destination) — p. 28

  7. 13 September 1872

    Homestead

    John files for a homestead

    John applies at Lincoln for 80 acres in Elk Precinct, near the site of the Plasi church.

    People: John Kastl

    p. 28

    As printed
    • He applied for the homestead on 13 September 1872, at Lincoln, Nebraska, so he was in the state on that date. — p. 28

  8. 1873

    Moving on

    Thomas in Champaign, Illinois

    Thomas, by now in Champaign, Illinois, marries Augusta Johns there. He has a buggy factory in Champaign.

    People: Thomas Kastl, Augusta Johns

    p. 56 p. 8

    As printed
    • In 1873, Thomas married Augusta Johns in Champaign. — p. 56
    • Thomas eventually settled in Champaign, Illinois, where he had a buggy factory. — p. 8

  9. 6 July 1875

    Homestead

    Joseph proves up

    In his final homestead proof Joseph describes his log house: 16 by 32 feet, two doors and four windows.

    People: Josef Kastl

    p. 8

    As printed
    • July 6, 1875, in his final proof required under the homestead act, Joseph states he built a house of logs, 16x32, with two doors and four windows. — p. 8

  10. 1878–1879

    Homestead

    John becomes a citizen and proves up

    John receives his certificate of U.S. citizenship on 16 October 1878 and his final certificate for the homestead on 13 March 1879.

    People: John Kastl

    p. 28

    As printed
    • John received his final certificate for the land on 13 March 1879. He also received his certificate of U.S Citizenship on 16 October 1878. — p. 28

The 1880s

  1. 1887–1888

    Moving on

    To California: Mary Kastl and Joseph Cuda

    Peter's daughter Mary marries Joseph Cuda at Plasi in January 1887 and moves with him to Hanford, California, the same year. In 1888 Cuda homesteads 160 acres in Kern County.

    People: Mary Kastl, Joseph Cuda

    p. 13

    As printed
    • Mary Kastl, the second daughter of Peter Kastl, born 1865 in Bohemia, married Joseph Cuda at Plasi, Nebraska, January 1887. That same year they moved to Hanford, California. — p. 13
    • The following year, 1888, Joseph Cuda homesteaded 160 acres of land in the San Joaquin Valley, Kern County, California. — p. 13

The 1890s

  1. 20 December 1890

    Death

    John dies

    John, the last brother to arrive, is the first whose death the book dates. He is buried at Plasi.

    People: John Kastl

    p. 28

    As printed
    • John died 20 December 1890, and was buried at Plasi, Saunders County, Nebraska. — p. 28

  2. 1891

    Moving on

    To Oklahoma: Mary B. Kastl and Vaclav Dobry

    Jacob's daughter Mary marries Vaclav Dobry and they move to the Oklahoma Territory as the Indian lands open for settlement.

    People: Mary Barbara Kastl, Vaclav A. Dobry

    p. 13

    As printed
    • Mary B. Kastl, the first daughter of Jacob Kastl, born March 1873, married Vaclav Dobry April 1891, and that same year they moved to the Oklahoma Territory. — p. 13

  3. 30 March 1899

    Moving on

    To California: Joseph Kastl's family

    Peter's son Joseph moves his family to Kern County, California, near the Cuda homestead.

    People: Joseph Kastl

    p. 13

    As printed
    • On March 30, 1899, the Joseph Kastl family moved to Kern County, California, and purchased land very near the Joseph Cuda homestead. — p. 13

The 1900s

  1. Early 1900s

    Moving on

    To Oklahoma and San Francisco

    Jacob's son Anton moves to the Oklahoma Territory (he marries Julia Hanska there in 1908). Wenzel's son John and daughter Anna move to the San Francisco area, and the family loses touch with them.

    People: Anton Peter Kastl, John Kastl, Anna Kastl

    p. 13 p. 26

    As printed
    • Anton P. Kastl moved to the Oklahoma Territory sometime in the early 1900s. There he married Julia Hanska in 1908 — p. 13
    • They moved to the San Francisco area in the early 1900s, and contact was lost by other relatives. — p. 26

  2. June 1902

    Moving on

    To Oklahoma: John J. Kastl

    Jacob's son John marries Frances Policky and moves to the Oklahoma Territory, buying land near the Dobrys.

    People: John Joseph Kastl, Frances Marie Policky

    p. 13

    As printed
    • John J. Kastl, first son of Jacob Kastl, married Frances Policky in June 1902, and moved to the Oklahoma Territory. — p. 13

  3. 1903–1906

    Death

    Peter, Thomas and Andrew die

    Peter dies on 31 October 1903, Thomas in Illinois on 30 September 1904, and Andrew on 5 May 1906.

    People: Peter Kastl, Thomas Kastl, Andrew Kastl

    p. 15 p. 56 p. 57

    As printed
    • Peter Kastl died 31 October 1903 — p. 15
    • Thomas Kastl died 30 September 1904. — p. 56
    • Andrew died 5 May 1906, and was buried at Plasi, Saunders County, Nebraska. — p. 57

The 1910s

  1. February 1913

    Moving on

    To Kansas: Barbara Kastl Mares

    John's daughter Barbara, who came over at six, moves with her husband Albert Mares and their family to a farm near Emporia, Kansas.

    People: Barbara Kastl, Albert Mares

    p. 31

    As printed
    • Albert and Barbara first lived on a farm near Dwight, Nebraska, but moved with their family to Kansas, near Emporia, February 1913. — p. 31

The 1920s

  1. 1921

    Death

    Josef dies

    Josef is buried at Brainard, Nebraska.

    People: Josef Kastl

    p. 41

    As printed
    • Josef died in 1921 and was buried at Brainard, Nebraska. — p. 41

  2. October 1922

    Arrival

    James Zima, from the second family

    Anna Kastl Zima's son James comes to Brainard, Nebraska, with the help of Vincent Kastl. He is the only one of Thomas Kastl's second family, or their descendants, to come to America.

    People: James Zima

    p. 62 p. 7

    As printed
    • James came to the United States in October 1922. He came to Brainard, Nebraska, with the help of an Uncle Vincent Kastl. — p. 62
    • The only descendant of the second family to come to America was James Zima. — p. 7

The 1930s

  1. 28 February 1935

    Death

    Jacob, the last of the brothers, dies

    Jacob dies at 87 at his son Frank's home near Bruno, having outlived all his brothers and his sister Eva. Where Wenzel died the book could not find out.

    People: Jacob Kastl, Wenzel Kastl

    p. 44 p. 26

    As printed
    • Jacob died 28 February 1935 and was buried at Plasi, Saunders County, Nebraska. — p. 44
    • Mr. Kastl had six brothers, Peter, Thomas, Joseph, Andrew, John, and Wencl, and one sister, Eva, all of whom passed away prior to the death of Grandpa Kastl. — p. 44
    • We have not been able to find much about Wenzel; where he died, or was buried. — p. 26
